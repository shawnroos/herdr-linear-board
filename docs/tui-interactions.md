# TUI interaction contract

This is the behavior-preservation source of truth for the functional UI redesign. Every listed capability must remain available through its existing keyboard path. Mouse/touch-first controls may expose the same capability, but must dispatch the same reducer/effect path and must not add product features.

## Layout vocabulary

- **Compact:** terminal width `< 60`; board shows exactly one full-width column and a four-row mobile header (identity/count, board + visibility controls, column navigator, divider). The navigator shows `(M)`/`(A)` beside the column and does not repeat the global running count. Sheets use only the board content region and receive a clickable `[ X ]`.
- **Regular:** width `60..=119`; multi-column board and centered content-region sheets, with the complete header on one line.
- **Wide:** width `>= 120`; same interaction model as Regular, with more columns/space and full filter names.
- Every layout reserves persistent top chrome and a bottom board action rail. There is no permanent footer hint row; a transient toast may use one row immediately above the action rail without changing idle geometry. Overlays and fullscreen detail/forms never cover the rails. Toasts expire after more than four seconds.
- Every visible button is an exact transparent `[ NAME ]` chip: only the chip cells paint bold white text and brackets; selected chips add an underline, and the surrounding background remains visible. Status cards retain semantic colors; primary/destructive action tone does not recolor buttons.
- In the tables, **❌ no mouse parity** means the keyboard capability cannot be performed by a corresponding mouse control. “Partial” calls out narrower mouse support.

## Linear mode

Linear mode is the board for a herdr space bound to a Linear project (see [`design.md`](design.md) section 13). It has its own screens and keys; the kanban rows above do not apply. The layout vocabulary differs: Linear mode has no Compact/Regular/Wide split. A page holds the pane width ÷ 48 columns, at least one and at most the workflow's, stretched to fill (`docs/responsive-board.md` Width). Cards cap at 64 cells and sit two to a row, 1 cell apart, once a column's inside reaches 81. When columns are hidden on a side, that side's outer gap becomes a `←` or `→` button with the hidden count under it, one tone above the board while selected; paging moves the page and never the selected issue. The "Narrow behavior" column below describes the one-column layout under 96 cells. A powerline tab row under the header names the snapshot's tabs with the active one on the accent; a document from before tabs has none. Column titles are one powerline bar, and lanes are one line each across the board (`─ ALPHA ── 5 󰈳 ─`), each band as tall as its tallest column (summed card heights and gaps) so lanes line up; a board with no lane grouping has no lane lines. Cards are sized to their content in two densities (`docs/board-design.md` §3). Roomy, the default, keeps padding, rules and blank rows and drops only the description and meta rows a card has nothing for: a full card is 13 rows, a title-only card 5. Compact drops the padding, rule and blank rows, and the blank row under lane lines: a full card is 8 rows, a title-only card 2. A 2-up row is as tall as its taller card. Selection changes only the surface, the title colour and, in roomy density, the two inner rules, so no text moves and no height changes; unfocused columns are muted, and the board scrolls as one unit to keep the selection in view (`tests/linear/layout.rs`). Agents' signals ride on the cards: the header shows every mark right-aligned in the order `!` needs you, `?` question, `◇` suggestion, `◉` show-request, `✓` done; a card with a note shows the latest one in place of its last description line as `› note`. The bottom bar holds global controls on the left (`keys`, `/ filter`, `esc deselect`, `ctrl+q quit`), a tip for the selection in the centre, and board-wide alerts on the right: one per pending show-request (oldest first, naming who asked and the issue), then `! N needs you`, `? N question`, then failing CI, one per PR. When the alerts do not fit, failing CI collapses to `✗ N CI failing`, then the bar shows the alerts that fit and `+N more`. When `gh` is unavailable one dim `gh unavailable` item replaces the CI alerts, so no CI count comes from missing data. Where the zones do not fit on one row the bar stacks into three centred rows: tip, alerts, controls (`tests/linear/bar.rs`). The labels of the other tabs add `!N` for hidden cards carrying `!`, `?`, `◇` or `◉`; the paging buttons show only how many columns they hide. A card's PR row shows `gh`'s preview, diff and CI once the daemon has read the PR, `…` while it has not, `gh unavailable` when `gh` is missing or failed for that PR, and dim placeholders from a daemon without `gh`. The board reads local state once after its first snapshot and after a reconnect, then on each change the daemon announces (`the_board_reads_local_state_after_its_first_snapshot_and_after_a_reconnect`, `ae6_a_sidebar_board_counts_attention_on_other_tabs`, `tests/linear/layout.rs::a_pr_gh_has_read_shows_its_preview_diff_and_ci`, `tests/linear/marks.rs::requests_at_44_cells_show_the_oldest_first_then_a_count_of_the_rest`). The cursor is held as tab, column, lane and identifier, and after every snapshot it is found again: the same lane, any lane of that column, any column, then the same position clamped (`a_snapshot_that_reorders_the_selected_column_keeps_the_cursor_on_the_same_identifier`, `a_selected_card_that_moved_columns_is_followed`, `a_selected_card_that_left_the_snapshot_leaves_the_cursor_in_the_same_column`). Columns are drawn in Linear's own progression — triage, backlog, unstarted, started, completed, canceled — from the state type the snapshot carries per group, and a column with no issues is drawn after every column that has one. A grouping that is not by workflow state carries no type, so those columns keep the view's order. ## Board levels

A Linear board that knows its own herdr pane (`HERDR_PANE_ID` and `HERDR_SOCKET_PATH`) picks its level from where that pane sits, through the daemon's `pane.context` read (KTD10). Alone in its tab, zoomed, or opened as an overlay (`scripts/open-board.sh`), it is the space board. As one split among several it shows the issue of the last focused split other than itself: that split's bound issue, else the first bound issue in the tab, else the space board. With no focus history it shows the tab's issue. The board re-reads on start, after a reconnect, on a `pane_layout_changed` for its workspace and on a `local_state_changed` for its space. A daemon without `pane.context` keeps the board at the space level and is not asked again. Tests are in `tests/linear/levels.rs` unless named otherwise.

## Mouse-parity summary

Mouse support is intentionally narrow rather than a full alternative input path. Full or useful parity exists for visible board/card focus, board card scrolling, detail popup/fullscreen, detail comment selection/edit/delete/history, form Save/Cancel, Compact sheet close, and Compact switcher rows. Board-card Edit/Delete are intentionally absent; the keyboard `e`/`d` paths remain. The largest explicit parity gaps are:

1. No mouse activation for Regular/Wide template application (board/project switching is clickable via the header `Board:`/`Project:` chips in every layout).
2. No mouse entry controls for create/edit/archive/delete/move/filter/refresh/run actions on the Board; Card Detail/comments retain their semantic Edit/Delete controls.
3. Confirmation yes/no choices are never clickable (picker rows are clickable and activate through the same `Enter` reducer).
4. Form fields cannot be focused, edited, or cycled with the mouse (only Save/Cancel).
5. Runs cannot be selected by clicking their rows; `o` has no click action.
6. Help and Comment History do not wheel-scroll.
7. Compact’s one-column board makes cross-column card/column drag effectively unavailable; keyboard `H/L` and `M` remain the routes.
8. Card Detail closes through its clickable title-row `[ X ]` control in every layout, matching the framed sheets.
